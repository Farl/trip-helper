# Analyze the exported binary preferences

Read the actual export schema and match `tripId`, `tripVersion`, `participantId` and `cardId` to the retained content version. Stop joining mismatched versions; report missing pack data rather than inventing a match. Never include invitation secrets, access tokens, or token hashes in analysis artifacts.

For an event log, reconstruct each participant/card's final accepted revision. A retry is not another vote. A current answer snapshot is already one answer per participant/card; do not sum it together with its history. Report the export time and participants included. If revoked participants are present, state whether they are excluded and why.

For every card retain counts for interested, not interested and unanswered. Give the denominator for any proportion: among answered or among all included participants. A blank answer does not mean dislike. Categories/tags may summarize patterns, but do not convert them into additional observed votes. The same place's separate experiences remain separate; liking a viewpoint does not imply liking shopping there.

Use direct, bounded statements such as “4 of 6 answered participants selected this card; 2 have not answered.” Describe patterns as suggestions within this pack, not established personal traits. A negative answer gives no reason: it cannot prove budget concerns, physical limitations, dietary needs or dislike of a whole destination. Ask for a relevant constraint when it materially affects the plan.

Propose shared options first, then compatible split-group or optional stops for mixed answers. Cite the card ID/title and its official source beside factual claims. Distinguish observed vote evidence from planning inference. State competing preferences explicitly, e.g. a stair-heavy viewpoint received mixed votes while a nearby indoor option is broadly selected.

Before turning preferences into an itinerary, recheck current opening dates, reservations, transport, prices, exhibit/event schedules, weather and mobility requirements with primary sources. Use the actual trip dates/year; an earlier card verification does not verify the eventual travel date. Do not promise future seasonal installations or guarantees of clear views.

Deliver the answer counts, a concise evidence-based reading, and proposed options with practical constraints. A group plan should remain editable and transparent about whose preferences it accommodates. Publishing, booking, or sending an analysis to others requires the corresponding user authorization.
